About

I am an assistant professor at the School of Management, Huazhong University of Science and Technology, one of China's top ten universities. I received my Ph.D. in Operations and Decision Technologies from the Paul Merage School of Business, UC Irvine, where I was advised by Professor Luyi Gui.

My research uses game-theoretic and optimization methods to study new business models in digital commerce and retail. It focuses on two areas: livestream commerce and the creator economy, as well as socially responsible operations.

In the first area, I study how influencers and brands jointly negotiate contract terms; how livestreaming shapes pricing and consumer behavior; and how platforms use their control over visibility to balance organic and sponsored content and to manage their creators' growth. I also study information design for livestream consultations on online medical platforms.

My second area, socially responsible operations, overlaps with the first in my work on rural livestream commerce, where I study who captures its gains and when competition among smallholders for attention gives rise to origin fraud. I also study retail operations for food recovery, such as pricing strategies at surplus-food supermarkets.

I hold a B.S. in Applied Mathematics from UCLA and an M.S. in Interdisciplinary Statistics and Operations Research from UNC Chapel Hill.

Upcoming: I will present "Surplus Food Retailing with Pay-What-You-Want Pricing" at the 2026 INFORMS Annual Meeting in San Francisco on Tuesday, November 3.
